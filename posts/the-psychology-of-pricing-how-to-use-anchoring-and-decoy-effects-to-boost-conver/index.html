The Psychology of Pricing: How to Use Anchoring and Decoy Effects to Boost Conversions on Shopify

2026-07-08 · Marketing · Tags: ecommerceempire, marketing, The

In the world of e-commerce, the price you set is never just a number. It's a psychological signal that shapes how customers perceive your products, your brand, and the value of what you're offering. While many store owners focus on product sourcing, ad spend, and site speed, the subtle art of pricing often gets overlooked. Yet, mastering pricing psychology can be the difference between a store that scrapes by and one that consistently converts visitors into paying customers. On Shopify, where you have complete control over product variants, bundles, and pricing tiers, you have a unique opportunity to implement proven psychological strategies that nudge buyers toward a purchase. In this article, we'll dive deep into two powerful techniques—anchoring and the decoy effect—and show you exactly how to apply them to your Shopify store to boost conversions and increase average order value.

The Power of Anchoring: Setting the Reference Point

Anchoring is a cognitive bias where individuals rely heavily on the first piece of information they receive (the "anchor") when making decisions. In pricing, the first price a customer sees becomes the reference point for all subsequent judgments. For example, if you see a jacket priced at $200, a $120 jacket suddenly seems like a bargain, even if $120 is still a premium price. This is why high-end retailers often display their most expensive items first or prominently—they're setting the anchor to make everything else look more affordable.

On Shopify, you can leverage anchoring in several ways. The most straightforward method is to show a higher-priced item next to a lower-priced one. For instance, if you sell watches, display a luxury edition at $300 alongside your standard edition at $150. The $300 price anchors the customer's perception of value, making the $150 watch appear to be a steal. But anchoring doesn't have to be limited to product comparisons. You can also use it within a single product page by showing a "compare at" price (the original price) struck through, with your current price below it. This is a classic anchor that creates a sense of urgency and savings.

To implement this effectively on Shopify, make sure you use the built-in "Compare at price" field in your product settings. When you set a compare-at price that's significantly higher than your selling price, customers see the discount immediately. But be careful—if the anchor is too high, it can seem unrealistic and erode trust. A good rule of thumb is to set the compare-at price at 20-40% higher than your selling price, depending on your niche. For high-ticket items, a smaller percentage works better; for impulse buys, a larger discount can be more compelling.

The Decoy Effect: Guiding Customers to Your Preferred Choice

The decoy effect, also known as the asymmetric dominance effect, occurs when consumers change their preference between two options when a third, less attractive option is presented. The classic example is the movie popcorn pricing: small for $3, medium for $6.50, and large for $7. The medium is the decoy—it's priced so close to the large that it makes the large look like a much better deal, even though most people wouldn't have considered the large otherwise. In e-commerce, you can use decoy pricing to steer customers toward a specific product or bundle that yields higher margins.

On Shopify, you can create decoy effects through product variants or bundles. Suppose you sell a digital course. You have a basic version for $49 and a premium version for $199. Many customers might hesitate at the $199 price. But if you introduce a third option—a "standard" version at $149 that offers slightly more than the basic but less than the premium—the premium suddenly looks more valuable. The $149 option acts as a decoy, making the $199 premium appear to be a smart upgrade for just $50 more. The key is to price the decoy so that it's close to the target option, making the target seem like the best value.

Another common decoy strategy is the "good, better, best" pricing model. You offer three tiers: a budget option, a mid-tier (the decoy), and a premium option. The mid-tier is priced just slightly below the premium, but with significantly fewer features. This pushes customers to choose the premium because they perceive it as the best value. To implement this on Shopify, use product variants with different prices and descriptions. Ensure that the decoy is not too cheap—it should be close enough to the premium that the premium seems like a logical upgrade. Also, highlight the premium option as "Most Popular" or "Best Value" to further influence the decision.

Practical Tactics for Implementing Pricing Psychology on Shopify

Now that you understand the core concepts, let's get into the nitty-gritty of how to apply them to your store. Here are some actionable tactics that go beyond just setting prices.

  • Use price anchoring in your product collections: When you display multiple products on a collection page, order them from highest to lowest price. This way, the first product a customer sees is the most expensive, setting an anchor that makes the rest seem more affordable. You can do this by customizing your collection sorting in Shopify.
  • Create bundle offers that leverage decoy pricing: Instead of selling individual products, create bundles with three options. For example, a single product at $20, a two-pack at $35 (the decoy), and a three-pack at $45. The two-pack is priced so close to the three-pack that customers will almost always choose the three-pack, increasing your average order value.
  • Show the original price before the sale price: Always display the compare-at price, even if it's not a traditional sale. You can use a strikethrough to show the original price and then your current price. This creates a sense of value and urgency, especially if you add a countdown timer for the sale.
  • Use charm pricing with a twist: While $9.99 is common, you can use psychological pricing that ends in .95 or .97 to appear more precise and trustworthy. However, combine this with anchoring—for example, show a product at $19.97 with a compare-at price of $29.95. The anchor makes the price seem even more attractive.
  • Implement price anchoring in your checkout page: If you offer a free shipping threshold, show the amount needed to reach free shipping. This acts as an anchor, encouraging customers to add more items to their cart. For example, "You're $15 away from free shipping"—the $15 becomes a small anchor that motivates an additional purchase.

These tactics are easy to implement on Shopify with the right apps or custom code. For instance, you can use apps like "Bundle Bear" or "Frequently Bought Together" to create bundle offers with decoy pricing. For collection page ordering, you can manually sort products or use a custom theme setting.

Avoiding Common Pitfalls in Pricing Psychology

While anchoring and decoy effects are powerful, they can backfire if not executed carefully. Here are some common mistakes to avoid.

First, don't set your anchor too high. If your compare-at price is 5x your selling price, customers will suspect you're inflating the original price just to make a sale. This erodes trust and can lead to negative reviews. Instead, keep your anchors realistic—ideally within 20-40% of your selling price. Second, avoid using too many decoys. If you offer five or six pricing tiers, customers can become overwhelmed and suffer from analysis paralysis. Stick to three options at most—a good, better, and best—to keep the decision simple. Third, don't ignore the value of your product. Pricing psychology can nudge customers, but it can't compensate for a poor product or a weak value proposition. Ensure your product descriptions and images reinforce the quality and benefits that justify the price.

Another pitfall is inconsistency. If you use anchoring on one product but not on a similar product, customers might notice and feel manipulated. Apply these principles consistently across your store. Finally, always test. What works for one audience might not work for another. Use Shopify's built-in A/B testing tools or apps like "Google Optimize" to experiment with different price points and compare conversion rates. Don't assume that a high anchor always works—sometimes a lower anchor with a smaller discount can be more effective if your audience is price-sensitive.

Case Study: A Real-World Example of Decoy Pricing in Action

To illustrate the impact of these strategies, let's look at a hypothetical Shopify store selling subscription boxes for coffee. The store owner initially offered two options: a monthly box at $15 and a quarterly box at $40. The conversion rate was decent, but most customers chose the monthly box, resulting in lower lifetime value. After learning about the decoy effect, the owner introduced a third option: a bi-monthly box at $35. Now, the pricing structure was:

  • Monthly: $15
  • Bi-monthly (decoy): $35
  • Quarterly: $40

Notice that the bi-monthly is priced at $35, which is just $5 less than the quarterly. The quarterly offers three boxes for $40, while the bi-monthly offers only two boxes for $35. The quarterly is clearly a better value per box ($13.33 vs $17.50), but the bi-monthly serves as a decoy to make the quarterly look like a steal. As a result, a significant portion of customers switched to the quarterly subscription, increasing the average order value by 167% (from $15 to $40). The owner also added a compare-at price for the quarterly box, showing it as "$60 value" to anchor the perceived worth.

This example demonstrates how a simple addition of a decoy option can dramatically shift customer behavior without any changes to the product itself. On Shopify, you can easily set up subscription products with variants or use apps like "Recharge" to manage subscription pricing tiers. The key is to design your pricing architecture with intention, not just list prices arbitrarily.

Conclusion

Pricing is not just a number—it's a psychological lever that can influence every decision a customer makes on your Shopify store. By mastering anchoring and the decoy effect, you can guide shoppers toward higher-value purchases, increase your average order value, and ultimately boost your revenue. Start by auditing your current pricing structure. Are you using compare-at prices effectively? Do you have multiple product tiers that create a decoy? If not, it's time to experiment. Remember, these strategies are not about manipulation; they're about presenting your products in a way that helps customers feel confident in their choices. When done right, pricing psychology benefits both you and your customers—they get a better deal, and you get a healthier bottom line. So go ahead, tweak your pricing, run some tests, and watch your conversion rates climb.
